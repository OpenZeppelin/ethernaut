`FlashQuorum` checks voting power the same way you'd check anything else on-chain: read the current balance, compare it to a threshold. That's exactly the problem. A balance is a snapshot of right now, and a flash loan can make "right now" say whatever the borrower needs it to say, for exactly as long as their transaction is running. There's no cooldown, no lockup, no proof that the tokens were held for more than one call.

This isn't hypothetical. Flash-loaned governance tokens were used to pass and immediately execute a malicious proposal against Beanstalk Farms in April 2022, draining the protocol's treasury in a single transaction. The fix that real governance systems converged on is checkpointing: voting power is recorded (usually at the block a proposal is created, or earlier) and proposals are voted on using that historical snapshot, not a live balance. OpenZeppelin's `ERC20Votes` and `Governor` contracts implement exactly this, on purpose.

If you're building anything that grants privileges based on a token balance, ask whether that balance could exist only because someone borrowed it a moment ago. If the answer is yes, you need a snapshot, not a read.

Notice that the 0.3% flash loan fee, the same rate Uniswap V2 charges on a flash swap, didn't stop the attack. The fee is real, but it's tiny next to what a passed proposal can release, and it has nothing to do with governance safety: it's a cost of borrowing, not a cost of voting. A flash loan being economically inexpensive for the attacker is a separate problem from a governance system that never checks how long a balance has been held. Fixing the second problem is what actually closes this hole; the fee alone never would have.
